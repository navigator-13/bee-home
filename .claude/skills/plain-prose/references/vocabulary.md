# Watchlist

Words that appear at much higher rates in machine-written text than in
human-written text. None is banned outright - `robust` is fine about a data
structure, `key` is fine about a keyboard. The test is whether the word is
doing work a plainer one would not.

Merged from the watchlists in `kylehughes/writing-prose-like-a-human-for-agents`
and `Byk3y/no-slop`, restated with replacements.

## Significance inflation

The largest single category, and the one that gives the game away fastest.
These tell the reader the thing was important instead of showing it.

pivotal, crucial, vital, essential, key (figurative), testament to,
watershed, turning point, indelible mark, lasting legacy, deeply rooted,
rich history, groundbreaking, revolutionary, transformative, profound,
plays a significant role, at the heart of, marks a new era, in an era where

Fix: delete the clause. If the fact matters, the reader will see it.

## Dressed-up copulas

serves as, stands as, represents, boasts, features, constitutes

Fix: `is`, `has`, or name the verb.

## Structural tics

Additionally, Furthermore, Moreover (as sentence openers)
It is worth noting, It's important to remember, It should be noted
Not only X but Y
From A to B (as a false range)
Despite these challenges
Setting the stage for
At its core
A delicate balance
In today's fast-paced world
In summary, Overall, In conclusion
Challenges and Legacy, Future Outlook (as headings)

## Vague attribution

experts say, studies suggest, industry reports indicate, critics argue,
observers note, several sources, it is widely regarded

Fix: name who, or cut the claim.

## Trailing participles

Anything of the shape `..., highlighting X` / `..., reflecting Y` /
`..., underscoring Z` / `..., paving the way for W`.

Fix: full stop after the fact. If the follow-on matters, give it its own
sentence and say it outright.
