Design

Design your very own Bee Home in a few minutes.

Just select size, visual style and desired placement — a rooftop, a balcony or a garden — or play around with the shuffle button. Your design will instantly update.

Every combination is one that can actually be cut and assembled. Storeys that would not physically stack are simply unavailable.

Download & fabricate

Download the design files for free and share them with a makerspace.

We'll help you find a local space, and they'll help you create your Bee Home. Most makerspaces have computer-controlled tools, including a CNC milling machine, which is what you'll need to fabricate it.

A makerspace is an open workshop with access to a variety of fabrication tools. Think of a gym, but for makers, where you either have a membership or pay per visit.

Place it

Facing the morning sun, within 300 metres of flowers.

Place it anywhere, as long as it's outside. Make sure your Bee Home is protected from strong winds and that the holes remain horizontal at all times.

Assembling it requires just your hands and a few minutes of your time. To mount it on a wall, simply use a drill and two screws.

How long a Bee Home lasts, depending on the wood, the location, and how well you clean and maintain it.

20–30

Offspring per queen — and every female solitary bee is a queen.

Plant for them

Since bees are free-living beings, we cannot guarantee their arrival.

Nonetheless, planting native wildflowers will definitely increase your chances. Tell us roughly where you are and we'll name the cavity-nesting bees recorded nearby, what they pollinate locally, and which seed to sow.

Solitary bees are friendly — they don't produce honey and therefore have nothing to protect. They only sting if trod on, and are safe to keep around kids and pets.
