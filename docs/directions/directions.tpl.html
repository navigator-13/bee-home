Bee Home revival · report 02 · visual directions

Three ways
to look at it

Each block below is built in its own direction rather than described in one — the palette, the type, the imagery and the motion are the proposal. The frame around them is deliberately neutral so they can be compared rather than harmonised.

Two of the three visuals are made from real project material: the bee-vision image is the original launch photograph run through a red-blind, UV-shifted transform, and the toolpaths are all 792 actual cutting curves lifted out of BEEHOME GEOMETRIES.3dm.

C · Field Guide is dropped, as you asked. For the record it was the weakest of the four anyway: pressed plants and a warm serif is where every sustainability project lands, and it was the one direction that would have made this look like something rather than like itself.

Direction A

The nest
section

Take the one thing that is genuinely distinctive and make it the whole structure: the tunnel. A solitary bee experiences this object as a dark corridor with a bright mouth.

Scrolling moves you down a borehole. Content sits in chambers that alternate left and right the way brood cells alternate along a nesting tube — so the asymmetry is structural rather than styled. Light comes from one side only and falls off fast.

The 3D lives at the bottom of the shaft: you arrive at the builder having travelled through the thing you are about to design.

Direction B

Bee vision

Human visionBee vision

The studio researched how bees interpret colour and then put almost none of it on screen. This direction makes that research the identity.

Bees are red-blind and see into the ultraviolet. Drag the image: that is the same photograph, with the red receptor removed and the short wavelengths pushed up. The warm timber the whole project is built from is, to its intended occupant, cold.

So the palette is violet, ultramarine and chartreuse, with wood as the only warm note on the page — and every warm thing reads as a deliberate address to humans rather than to bees. Scroll transitions flip photographs between the two ways of seeing.

Direction D

Stock & toolpath

$TOOLPATHS
CUT-OUTSIDE_T6MM_30.00MM POCKET-INSIDE_T6MM_20.00MM

Honeycomb, but earned. Not hexagon wallpaper — the actual nesting of parts on a sheet of stock.

Those are all 792 real cutting curves from the project's own Rhino file, in the project's own layer colours. The vocabulary is already sitting there: red for outside cuts, magenta for pockets, layer names that state material thickness and depth to two decimal places. Nothing needs inventing.

On scroll the toolpaths draw themselves in cut order. Layout is asymmetric because nesting is asymmetric — parts pack around each other and the leftover is visible, which is the honest picture of making something from a sheet.

Palette
Type
Monospace throughout — the machine's own voice. Set small, set tight, never apologised for.
Motion
Paths drawing on scroll; a live nesting preview as storeys are added.
Risk
Cold, and it speaks to people who already own machines — the exact audience the project needs to stop speaking to exclusively.
B + D

The hybrid, which is the one I'd build

The two halves answer different questions, and the site has exactly two questions to answer. Split it at the point where a visitor stops being an audience and starts being a maker — the moment they open the builder.

Before the builder · B

Why bees, why this, why you. Violet and UV, vision-swap photography, the display face at scale. Emotional, strange, memorable.

Everything here is arguing that the world looks different to the animal you are building for.

From the builder on · D

Configure, nest, cut, assemble, maintain. Bone and mono and hairlines — which is also exactly what the original builder looked like, so this half is a restoration rather than an invention.

The switch is the reward. Crossing into it should feel like the lights coming on in a workshop.

It also solves a practical problem: the builder is the one screen we have real reference for, and D is that reference. Nothing has to be invented where evidence exists, and invention goes where there is none.

Ranked, with reasons

  1. B + D hybridOwnable, evidence-backed where evidence exists, and the split falls on a real seam in the user's intent rather than an arbitrary one.
  2. B aloneThe strongest single idea. Loses the fabrication honesty that makes the project more than a nice object.
  3. D aloneThe most authentic and the most narrowing. Good for the repo, weak for the public front door.
  4. AThe best photography direction of the three and the hardest to sustain. Worth keeping in reserve for one section — the nest explainer — rather than the whole site.
